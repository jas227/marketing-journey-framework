# Marketing Journey Framework copy-and-paste prompt

Replace the business details at the bottom, then paste the complete prompt into ChatGPT or Codex.

---

I want you to create a **[Company Name] Marketing Journey Map** using **The Marketing Journey Framework and its 18 Atomic Units of Marketing**.

Treat the map as a connected system of researched possibilities, hypotheses, strategic choices, constraints, and commitments. Do not present exploratory possibilities as established conclusions.

For an existing business, research current first-party sources and relevant independent customer or market evidence. Cite factual claims and distinguish sourced facts, reasonable inferences, and strategic hypotheses. For an early business idea, use the information I provide and label assumptions clearly.

Generate approximately three to six substantive possibilities for each atomic unit. Add more only when the distinctions are strategically meaningful.

Organize the map from left to right under these three supergroups:

- **Marketing Context:** Human Context and Decision Environment
- **Marketing Activities:** Market Engagement and Brand World
- **Offer:** Value Proposition and Pricing and Proof

Use these six groups and 18 atomic units in this exact order:

1. **Human Context:** ICPs/Personas, Category Entry Moments, Psychological States
2. **Decision Environment:** Buying Categories/Industries, Competitors/Alternatives, Influence Sources
3. **Market Engagement:** Channels, Informational Content, Experiential Content
4. **Brand World:** Stories/Narratives, Aesthetic Directions, Language Directions
5. **Value Proposition:** Benefits, Features, Purchase Categories
6. **Pricing and Proof:** Pricing Options/Structures, Quantitative Social Proof, Qualitative Social Proof

Apply these definitions carefully:

- **ICPs/Personas:** The people or organizations being prioritized, including users, buyers, champions, blockers, and relevant account types.
- **Category Entry Moments:** Concrete events or changes that activate consideration of a buying category.
- **Psychological States:** The emotions, motivations, beliefs, risks, identity tensions, and readiness that accompany the entry moment.
- **Buying Categories/Industries:** The broad mental, budgetary, or industry universes in which competitors and alternatives live.
- **Competitors/Alternatives:** Direct competitors, substitutes, DIY, hiring someone, delaying, and doing nothing.
- **Influence Sources:** People, communities, institutions, experts, media, internal stakeholders, and algorithms shaping the decision.
- **Channels:** The specific contexts where marketing moments happen, including particular platforms, publications, events, referrals, sales interactions, retailers, and product experiences.
- **Informational Content:** Useful media about the problem, category, alternatives, surrounding life situation, or purchase decision that does not center the company’s own products or services.
- **Experiential Content:** Media, interactions, and activations that create feeling, aspiration, participation, enjoyment, belonging, or memorability.
- **Stories/Narratives:** The brand’s larger worldview, purpose, beliefs, interpretation of change, and desired future.
- **Aesthetic Directions:** Broad vibes, cultural references, emotional atmospheres, identity signals, and status signals.
- **Language Directions:** Tone, vocabulary, cadence, point of view, rhetorical posture, and verbal conventions.
- **Benefits:** Functional, economic, emotional, social, and identity-level transformations.
- **Features:** Concrete capabilities, services, components, experiences, and mechanisms producing those benefits.
- **Purchase Categories:** Customer-recognizable families of products, services, plans, or offers sold by the brand.
- **Pricing Options/Structures:** Price levels, pricing metrics, packaging, commitments, terms, financing, discounting, guarantees, and risk reduction.
- **Quantitative Social Proof:** Numerical evidence supporting important claims, with each metric tied to the claim it supports.
- **Qualitative Social Proof:** Testimonials, cases, reviews, recognizable customers, press, endorsements, referrals, or institutional validation tied to the claim supported.

After building the possibility map, identify strong connections, contradictions, unsupported assumptions, and consequential unknowns. Provide a concise provisional strategy spine selecting one coherent configuration across the framework.

Create exactly three distinct **Customer Marketing Journeys**. Each should follow a believable customer from category entry and psychological response through consideration, discovery, brand interpretation, offer evaluation, pricing, proof, purchase, and intended outcome. Use meaningfully different personas, entry moments, buying categories, or routes to market. Write compact customer stories rather than labeled checklists. Retain an explicit list of the exact map-option IDs used by each journey.

When interactive visualization is supported, create a responsive left-to-right map. Show all possibilities initially with no journey selected. Add controls for **All Possibilities**, **Journey 1**, **Journey 2**, and **Journey 3**. Selecting a journey should softly highlight only its relevant possibilities while keeping everything else visible but secondary. Do not add relationship lines or a permanent selected-item summary pill. Place each atomic unit in a subtle outlined container. Add concise hover or tap descriptions for every supergroup, group, atomic-unit heading, and company-specific possibility. Prevent text overlap at every screen width and allow horizontal scrolling when necessary. Validate that every journey references existing option IDs and that the visualization loads without errors.
